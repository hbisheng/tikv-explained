# RAFTSTORE 802: Raft Hibernation

Raft needs time to move forward even when no client is writing data. Each peer receives a periodic **tick**. A tick advances Raft's logical clock: it drives heartbeats, election timeouts, and other timer-based work.

In the usual configuration, this base tick happens once per second. That is cheap for one Region, but a store may host a very large number of idle Regions. Processing ticks for every idle peer can consume a meaningful part of Raftstore's worker time.

**Hibernation** lets a stable, idle Raft group slow down its base ticks without weakening failure detection.

## A Group Must Be Truly Idle

Hibernation is not just a local switch on one peer. The whole Raft group must be quiet:

- no writes have arrived for a while;
- no requests are waiting;
- no leader transfer or similar administrative work is in progress;
- no peer has work that could change the group's state.

The leader checks the group-wide conditions because it has the clearest view of the Raft group. A follower checks its own local conditions.

The leader starts a coordination round. Followers agree only when their local conditions also allow hibernation. The group enters hibernation after a quorum agrees.

The initial check can be relaxed: it is acceptable to occasionally ask whether a group is idle. The final transition must be strict. A request or state change during that transition means the group must remain active.

## Entering Hibernation

Raftstore checks the conditions on both sides of a tick. One tick can change the very conditions being tested: a follower may begin an election, or a leader may step down.

This is why the code has both `check_before_tick()` and `check_after_tick()`.

The leader first enters a `Polling` state. It does not become fully idle until the next check, after another 10-second period. This extra interval makes sure that no command woke the Region while the group was negotiating hibernation.

Only then does the group enter `Idle` and reduce normal Raft tick processing.

## Staying Safe While Idle

Hibernation does not mean that a follower forgets how to detect a failed leader. It uses a slower stale check.

The leader sends a heartbeat during this check roughly every five minutes. A healthy follower can remain idle, while a follower that stops hearing from the leader gradually returns to normal Raft ticking.

An idle follower has the following important state:

```text
group_state = Idle
Raft tick registered = false
missing_ticks = 6
```

`missing_ticks` controls how quickly a follower re-enables normal ticks after the idle checks stop hearing from its leader.

A healthy follower may move through these states for a long time:

```text
Idle -> PreChaos -> Ordered -> Idle
```

`PreChaos` does not necessarily mean that a network failure happened. The leader's and follower's five-minute timers can have different phases. A later leader heartbeat moves the follower back through `Ordered` and into `Idle`.

## When the Leader Really Fails

If the leader is gone, the follower does not receive the heartbeat that would return it to `Idle`.

```text
stale check 1: Idle     -> PreChaos
stale check 2: PreChaos -> Chaos
stale check 3: Chaos    -> register normal Raft tick
```

Once normal Raft ticking is registered again, the usual election timeout can expire and the follower can start an election.

The value of `missing_ticks = 6` is a balance. A follower should not wait through a full fresh election timeout after it wakes up, but it should leave enough time for a healthy leader to wake up and re-establish contact before starting an election.

## Waking a Hibernated Region

A client read or write wakes the Region. There is no reason to keep it asleep once it has real work to do.

The slow stale checks are for a different case: a Region that remains idle while its leader has failed. They make failure recovery possible without paying the normal tick cost continuously.

## Busy Apply Must Still Make Progress

Hibernation once exposed a progress problem for busy apply peers. Without base ticks, a peer might not be revisited, so apply work could remain busy without advancing.

The first fix attached a busy-peer recheck to applied results. That was not enough: a peer might have no new apply result at all. Rechecks were later added after the busy-apply path obtains the commit index, with another check before entering hibernation as a final safeguard.

The general lesson is that hibernation can remove a periodic source of progress. Every task that relied on that periodic work needs another explicit wake-up path.
