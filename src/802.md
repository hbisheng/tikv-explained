# RAFTSTORE 802: Raft Hibernation

[RAFTSTORE 701](701.md) showed how one TiKV store processes many Region peers. Batching makes that work efficient, but an idle peer still has one recurring task: the **Raft tick**.

A tick is a local timer event that advances Raft's logical clock. Leaders use it to send periodic heartbeats, while followers use it to decide when the election timeout has passed. With TiKV's default configuration, every active peer receives a base tick once per second.

```text
base tick
   |
   +----> leader: drive periodic heartbeats
   |
   +----> follower: advance the election timer
```

One tick per second is inexpensive for one peer. A store may host hundreds of thousands of Region peers, however, and most of them may be idle. Scheduling and processing all those ticks can consume significant Raftstore CPU time even when the Regions have no useful work.

**Raft hibernation** lets the peers of a stable Raft group stop their normal base ticks. It is a TiKV optimization around Raft: the Region keeps its existing leader and data, but Raftstore stops driving its Raft timers every second.

## When a Peer Can Hibernate

A follower can hibernate when it has a valid leader, its latest log entry belongs to the current term, and it has no unresolved read or snapshot work that requires more Raft progress.

The leader must check the whole group before it stops ticking. In particular:

- the available peers have caught up with the leader's latest log entry;
- the caught-up peers form a quorum;
- the leader has applied its latest log entry; and
- no read, leader transfer, or other unfinished work requires the group to keep moving.

These checks prevent the leader from going quiet while a healthy follower is still catching up or while committed work is waiting to be applied.

Raftstore checks the conditions both before and after advancing one tick. The tick itself may change the result: for example, the leader may step down. The peer hibernates only if the conditions still hold afterward.

## Agreeing to Hibernate

The leader does not stop ticking based only on its local view. It first polls the other peers:

```text
leader
  |
  | MsgHibernateRequest
  v
followers
  |
  | MsgHibernateResponse
  v
leader
```

A follower responds only when the request comes from its current leader, it has no uncommitted log entry, and it is not waiting for Region data from a snapshot.

The leader waits until every peer it still considers available has agreed. If some peers are already known to be down, their responses may be omitted, but the peers that agreed must still form a quorum.

Hibernation takes two eligible checks. The first starts this polling phase. At the next check, normally after another election-timeout interval, the leader verifies the conditions and responses again before becoming idle. This delay prevents a brief quiet moment from putting the group to sleep immediately.

Once the checks pass, the leader stops its normal base tick. A follower records several skipped ticks before it fully stops scheduling them. With the default election and heartbeat settings, it records six. When the follower wakes, Raftstore replays that count into `raft-rs`. Its election timer resumes closer to the timeout, while still leaving the leader time to send a heartbeat before followers begin competing in an election.

## Waking for Work

A Region does not wait for a periodic timer when work needs Raft to make progress. A new proposal, a ReadIndex request, or a relevant incoming Raft message wakes the peer and registers its normal base tick again.

```text
hibernated peer
      |
      | proposal, ReadIndex, or Raft message
      v
resume normal Raft ticks
```

The peer returns to the normal active state, processes the work, and may hibernate again after the group becomes stable.

## Detecting a Failed Leader

A Region can remain idle while its leader fails, so direct request wake-ups are not enough. Hibernated peers retain a much slower **stale check**, which runs every five minutes by default.

During its stale check, a hibernated leader sends a Raft heartbeat. Receiving that heartbeat prevents the followers from progressing toward an election.

If a follower no longer hears from the leader, it moves through two warning states before restoring the normal Raft tick:

```text
Idle
  |
  | first stale check
  v
PreChaos
  |
  | next stale check without a leader heartbeat
  v
Chaos
  |
  | next stale check
  v
resume normal Raft ticks and election detection
```

`PreChaos` is only a warning state. The leader's and follower's stale-check timers are not synchronized, so a healthy follower may briefly enter it before the leader's heartbeat arrives. That heartbeat returns the follower to the normal `Ordered` state, from which it can hibernate again.

If the leader is truly gone, no heartbeat resets the follower. The follower eventually reaches `Chaos`, resumes ordinary Raft ticks, and can start an election after its remaining election timeout passes.

This path deliberately detects failure more slowly than a continuously ticking Raft group. The tradeoff is worthwhile for idle Regions: TiKV preserves Raft safety and can recover when a quorum remains available, while avoiding constant tick work across the store.

---

The complete hibernation path is:

```text
stable Raft group
       |
       v
peers check local and group progress
       |
       v
leader gathers hibernation responses
       |
       v
normal Raft ticks stop
       |
       +----> Raft work or message: wake immediately
       |
       +----> leader failure: stale checks restore ticking
```

Hibernation is a small scheduling optimization, but it matters at TiKV's scale. Quiet Regions stop consuming steady Raftstore attention and become active again when there is work to do.
